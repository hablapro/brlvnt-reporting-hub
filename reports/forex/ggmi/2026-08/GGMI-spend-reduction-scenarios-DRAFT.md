## 2. What the model shows by channel

At current August cost-per-application levels, each channel models as follows. Actual volume will depend on how channel efficiency changes at the new weights.

| Channel | Controlled spend | Modeled applications | Deep spend | Modeled applications |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Bing | $25,000 | ~66 | $18,500 | ~49 |
| Azerion | $25,000 | ~26 | $15,000 | ~15 |
| Quantcast | $11,000 | Not modeled | $0 | n/a |
| Native | $0 | n/a | $0 | n/a |

**Channel-level modeled applications are shown independently and should not be summed, because attribution methods may overlap.** Bing application counts come from search platform attribution and Azerion's from the vendor's own reporting. A prospect who saw an Azerion display ad and later clicked a Bing search ad may be counted by both, and we have no deduplicated source-level figure that would let us net that out.

These are model outputs, not expected results. They hold each channel's current cost per application constant while moving Bing from about $18,500 to $25,000, and Bing's cost per application has already risen from about $237 to $379 as it scaled through August. It may rise again. Azerion at a lower weight may not hold $978 either. Read the figures as sizing.

The comparison that matters is between channels rather than in total: at $25,000 each, Bing models to roughly two and a half times the applications Azerion does. That ratio is the case for the weighting proposed here.

## 3. The reasoning behind the weights

Submitted applications are the most directly attributable acquisition outcome currently available at channel level. Funded accounts remain the ultimate business outcome, and wherever source-level funding data is available it should be used to validate channel quality.

| Line | August cost per submitted application | Detail |
|---|---:|---|
| **Bing** | **$379** | 41 applications on $15,543 through August 26 |
| **Azerion** | **$978** | 25 applications through August 17; about 44 projected for the month |
| Quantcast | Not reported | Reports results, not submitted applications |
| Native | None we can trace to it | Delivers site visits only |

We read each channel on the signal its own platform reports. The two application costs are not combined, and no cross-channel acquisition cost is shown, because the methods are not directly comparable.

### Azerion efficiency has declined as spend scaled

| Month | Spend | Applications | Cost per application |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| April | $11,236 | 19 | $591 |
| May | $22,158 | 31 | $715 |
| June | $28,002 | 32 | $875 |
| July | $37,509 | 41 | $915 |
| August | $42,956 | ~44 | **$978** |

Spend nearly quadrupled and the cost of each application rose at every step. The pattern is consistent with diminishing marginal efficiency as spend scales: additional budget continues to produce applications, but at progressively higher acquisition costs. August is a month-end projection with its application count derived from the $978 recorded across August 1 to 17.

Bing remains materially more efficient. Its cost per application increased from approximately $237 during the first ten days after the July 22 relaunch to $379 as volume scaled through August, but it remains less than half Azerion's current cost per application.

The proposed Native, Azerion and Quantcast changes can be executed with 24 hours' notice, with no obligation to make up cancelled delivery.

## 4. Controlled Pullback, $61,000

Sets Bing at $25,000, below its approved $30,000 and above the $18,500 it is currently running, so the channel with the strongest application economics takes a larger relative share.

Cuts Azerion from about $43,000 to $25,000, a 42% reduction, while keeping the line running, because it does produce applications at a cost we can measure.

Retains $11,000 in Quantcast as a limited test allocation. Whether it continues beyond the October review should depend on whether it can show measurable contribution beyond view-through reporting. It also shuts off an out-of-date Quantcast campaign that is spending about $9,451 a month and produced nothing in August.

Removes Native, which produces no applications we can trace back to it.

Defers the Meta, TikTok and Strategic Partnerships launches rather than cancelling them, so those channels can be reconsidered as part of the October review if the evidence supports activation.

### Why this is the recommendation

1. Azerion shows declining efficiency as spend scales.
2. Bing currently has substantially better application economics and deserves greater relative allocation.
3. Native shows no attributable application outcome that would justify continued spend during a pullback.
4. $11,000 keeps Quantcast as a measured test rather than a major budget line.
5. The Native, Azerion and Quantcast changes can be executed with 24 hours' notice.
6. September provides another month of actual market and conversion evidence before a Q4 decision.
7. The stablecoin launch remains an input, not the justification for the entire media strategy.

---

## 5. Deep Pullback, $33,500

Holds Bing at its current pace, cuts Azerion to $15,000, and removes Quantcast and Native entirely. Only the two channels that produce submitted applications continue.

It preserves the most cash and increases no channel. The cost is volume. Bing models to roughly 49 applications against 66 under the Controlled Pullback, and Azerion to roughly 15 against 26. It also leaves no display activity running, so restoring display later carries a larger operational and optimization ramp.

**Where the two differ.** Both keep the channels that produce applications and remove the one that does not. They differ on weight, and on whether $11,000 of measured Quantcast activity is worth retaining. Quantcast is the weakest line on the measure available to us: it reports results rather than submitted applications, and most of those results are credited to people who saw an ad rather than clicked one. Maintaining limited activity reduces the operational and optimization ramp required if display investment is restored.

## 6. Timing and the October review

Run the reduced plan through September. Review performance and market conditions in the first half of October. If the evidence supports a ramp, begin rebuilding display in mid-October and increase search closer to the late-October and November demand window.

We are not proposing that October stays suppressed. September is there to produce information.

The reason the review sits in early October rather than later is operational. Search can be increased close to an event, because someone typing a search is already looking. Display cannot: it carries booking, creative and optimization lead time before it delivers efficiently. A decision taken in November arrives too late to act on November.

Banco de Mexico meets two days later.

## 7. Considerations

**What this proposal does not yet establish.** We know Bing applications cost less than Azerion applications. We have not established that Bing applications fund at a better rate. Cost per application is the best channel-level signal available today; funded-account quality by source is the check that would confirm or overturn the weighting proposed here, and we recommend prioritising that data.

**Weak funding does not by itself identify media as the problem.** The channels are producing applications. If those applications are not funding, the cause may sit after the advertising rather than in it: a quiet market, less appetite to trade, difficulty completing sign-up, delays between approval and funding, the deposit methods on offer, the minimum required to fund, or how the product compares with competitors. This proposal is sized on what each channel costs to produce an application, not on a conclusion about which stage of the journey is failing.

**On the stablecoin launch.** The stablecoin launch may improve accessibility and broaden acquisition opportunities, but we do not yet have evidence that it will materially change funded-account conversion. We therefore recommend treating it as one potential catalyst rather than the sole condition for restoring media investment.

**Market indicators are context, not triggers.** The VIX measures expected US equity volatility rather than currency volatility, so it is only a proxy for the broad risk environment. For GGMI, CME's currency volatility indices are the better measure, and the Mexican peso index is more relevant than any stock-market one. None of the dates above is an automatic budget trigger.

**What we will report against**, in priority order: FOREX.com applications, approvals and funded accounts; cost per funded account where available, read with care where the denominators are small; Bing cost per submitted application at its new weight; Azerion cost per submitted application at its reduced weight; whether the Quantcast test can demonstrate contribution beyond view-through reporting; then market context.
