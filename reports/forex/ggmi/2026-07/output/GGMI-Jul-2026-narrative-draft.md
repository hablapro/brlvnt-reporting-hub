## Executive summary

July working media totaled $149,896 across six lines, detailed in the table below. Native and DOOH are new lines this month. Both stay in the deck so the totals foot to the tracker, and both report spend only until a second month of data gives them something to compare against.

The agency scorecard rests on Bing this month. Search delivered 20 submitted applications at $531.25 across the full month, and the account was live for barely half of it. July split into three windows: dark from the June pause through July 10, a legacy structure that ran July 11 to 22 and produced zero applications, and a rebuilt Mexico-only structure that launched July 22 and closed the month at $237.12 per submitted application, less than half June's $513.17. The rebuild is the story. August's first four days confirm it: $243 per application on a structure that is now converting across three products instead of one.

Two items carry real weight into August. First, Mexico-only delivery improved sharply, from roughly half of Bing's spend serving outside Mexico in June to 2.7% in July, but the setting that caused the earlier leak is unchanged and Venezuela is still not excluded. The gap is dormant, not closed, and it returns as the rebuilt campaigns scale. Second, the legacy campaigns that spent $5,883 for zero applications sent 84% of their clicks to pages that were not part of the application flow; the rebuilt campaigns, landing on the Spanish-language site instead, carried 13.4% of their starts through to a submitted application. Standardizing every campaign on the converting destination is the highest-leverage fix on the table for August.

## Why it happened

### Search (Bing): the account that came back mid-month

Bing spent $10,625 across three distinct windows in July, and averaging them into one monthly figure would hide the entire story.

- **July 1 to 10, dark.** No delivery, carried over from the June pause.
- **July 11 to 22, the legacy structure.** $5,883 spent, zero submitted applications.
- **July 22 to 31, the rebuilt Mexico-only structure.** $4,742 spent, 20 submitted applications at $237.12 each, all from two new campaigns.

The rebuild is what moved the number. The full-month cost per submitted application, $531.25, blends nine dead days and eleven zero-conversion days into the twenty that actually delivered, so it understates what the rebuilt structure is now doing on its own. Read the second half of July, not the average of the month, as the account's current state. August's first four days back that read: $243 per submitted application on $1,459 of spend, with two additional products now converting alongside the one that carried July.

Two campaigns produced every July conversion: the TradingView-themed campaign contributed 18 of the 20 applications, and a second new campaign contributed the remaining 2.

**Why the legacy campaigns converted at zero.** The diagnostic that separates the two structures is where the clicks landed, not just when they ran. The legacy campaigns sent 84% of their July clicks to pages outside the application flow, mainly the corporate overview page and two older landing pages, and every one of their 207 application starts stopped at the second step of the funnel with no exceptions. The rebuilt campaigns landed on the Spanish-language site instead, and the TradingView campaign alone carried 13.4% of its starts through to a submitted application. The landing destination and the keyword intent behind each campaign changed at the same time, so this is not a proven single cause, but it points at the same conclusion the client's own Q2 review reached: the loss sits in the handoff from a started application to a submitted one, not in top-of-funnel volume.

**Mexico-only delivery: improved, not resolved.** July's out-of-market spend was $285 of $10,625, or 2.7%, down from close to half of the account in June. That improvement came from pausing the leaking legacy campaigns on July 22, not from a targeting fix. Every enabled campaign in the account, including the six new Mexico-only campaigns, still runs on a setting that targets by presence or interest rather than presence alone, and Venezuela, the largest source of out-of-market delivery in both June and July, is still not on the exclusion list. The gap is dormant this month because volume is low. It is not fixed, and it returns as the rebuilt campaigns scale into August and beyond. Fixing it is the top priority in the recommendations below.

### Programmatic: Quantcast, reach at a viewability cost

Quantcast spent $39,240 against 50,483,289 impressions and 19,188 clicks, all within Mexico; the geo cut confirms 100.00% of spend and impressions delivered in-market. Viewability came in at 54.45%, still well under the 70% floor the account works against. The lever is the site list: a refreshed disallow list covers 66 domains this month. One site accounts for a disproportionate share of the problem on its own. Spend on tvazteca.com grew to $3,451.61 in July while viewability on that domain held near 9.6%, the second consecutive month the pattern has repeated. If the client actions a single exclusion this month, that is the one.

Quantcast's result volume this month (15) is too low to support a cost-per-result figure, so the channel stays in the deck as an awareness and reach story, not a conversion story.

### Programmatic: Azerion, steady on efficiency, viewability passing

Azerion spent $37,509 against 7,577,455 impressions and 12,915 clicks, and the vendor reports 41 submitted applications at $914.85 each. These are vendor-reported figures, not CRM-validated the way Bing's count is, and the deck states that distinction every time the number appears. Viewability passed the 70% floor on both formats the vendor delivers: 82.47% on display, 77.34% on native.

### Social (Meta): spend and delivery only this month

Meta spent $8,027 against 4,250,494 impressions and 91,153 clicks, a 2.14% click-through rate and roughly $1.89 CPM. Reach on the largest campaign came to 2,251,346; frequency and reach are not summed across the account's other campaigns because Meta does not de-duplicate reach across campaigns in reporting. Delivery held to Mexico across every campaign.

Placement mix shifted toward Instagram: 10.2% of July delivery ran there, up from 0.3% in June, alongside Facebook at 89.2% and small shares on Audience Network, Threads and Messenger. That answers the one specific ask the client made in the June review. The other ask, narrowing targeting away from the 55-and-older audience, was not implemented; targeting still runs 18 to 65 with Meta's own audience expansion turned on.

This month's Meta reporting covers spend and delivery only: impressions, reach, frequency, clicks, click-through rate, cost per click, cost per thousand impressions, landing-page views, placements and geo. Meta's platform reports 117 submitted-application pixel fires in July; that figure is Meta-reported and unvalidated, is not compared to Bing's CRM-validated count, and does not carry a cost-per-application figure of its own.

### Site traffic (GA4, Mexico): context, not conversion

Mexico sessions came to 7,310 in July, down from June's 9,236, and unique visitors came to 3,981. Read alongside Bing's own month: the account was dark for the first ten days and running a non-converting legacy structure for the next eleven, so a large share of July's session dip tracks the search channel's own gap rather than a broader traffic decline. Property-wide, the headline session count looks worse than the underlying pattern because one unattributed traffic bucket collapsed sharply month over month; sessions outside that bucket actually grew.

The tracking gap that limited what GA4 could confirm this month is fixed. The event that should record a submitted application as a key event started counting correctly on the last day of July and has held at full capture every day since. July still has to be reported on Bing's own CRM count because the fix landed with one day of the month left. GA4 cannot independently corroborate what Bing reports until August.

Venezuela's session volume from Mexico's Spanish-language site fell sharply in July, tracking the same legacy-campaign pause that drove Bing's out-of-market spend down. That is a delivery-volume effect of the pause, the same caveat as the Bing geo finding above: it is not evidence that the underlying targeting setting changed.

## What to do next

1. **Fix the Mexico targeting setting before Bing scales further.** Every enabled campaign, including the six new ones, still targets by presence or interest rather than presence alone, and Venezuela is not on the exclusion list. July's 2.7% out-of-market spend ($285 of $10,625) is low only because volume is low and the leaking legacy campaigns were paused; the setting itself did not change. This is the top priority for August.
2. **Standardize every Bing campaign on the converting landing destination.** The legacy campaigns sent 84% of clicks to pages outside the application flow and produced zero of their 207 application starts as submissions; the rebuilt campaigns landing on the Spanish-language site carried 13.4% of starts through to submission. Review the handoff from the legacy landing pages to the application flow and bring every active campaign onto the destination that is converting.
3. **Turn on bidding feedback for the Bing conversion goals.** Every conversion goal in the account is still excluded from bidding, and every campaign runs on manual bids, so July's $237.12 cost per submitted application on the rebuilt structure was earned with no automated signal at all. Enabling the goals for bidding gives the platform the data it needs to improve on that number without manual tuning.
4. **Apply the refreshed 66-site Quantcast disallow list, leading with tvazteca.com.** Spend on that single domain grew to $3,451.61 in July at roughly 9.6% viewability, the second month running. Closing that gap is the fastest lever against the account's 54.45% viewability against a 70% target.
5. **Hold Azerion's pace.** Viewability passed on both display (82.47%) and native (77.34%) formats, and cost per application ($914.85 on the vendor's own reporting) is in line with prior months.
