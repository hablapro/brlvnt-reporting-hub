## Executive summary

July working media totaled $149,896, up 24% from June's $120,393 and the biggest GGMI media month of 2026, the same milestone June's own deck claimed for itself at the time. Six lines make up the total this month; Native and Digital Out of Home (DOOH) are new, detailed below.

The agency scorecard rests on Bing and Azerion, the two channels with a submitted-application figure behind them. Bing delivered 20 submitted applications at $531.25 across the full month; Azerion delivered 41 at $914.85 on the vendor's own reporting. Combined, the two channels produced 61 submitted applications at $789.08 each, against June's 92 at $660.00, the same combined view June's deck introduced and the same footnote: Quantcast's view-through results and Meta's platform-reported figures are not part of this count. The rise in cost per application traces to Bing's slow start, not to Azerion, which held steady.

Bing's month split into three windows: dark from the June pause through July 10, a legacy structure that ran July 11 to 22 and produced zero applications, and a rebuilt Mexico-only structure that launched July 22 and closed the month at $237.12 per submitted application, less than half June's $513.17. The rebuild is the story, and August's first four days confirm it: $243 per application on a structure now converting across three products instead of one.

June's deck committed to redirecting roughly $8.6K a month of Bing spend that had been serving outside Mexico back into the market. That commitment held through July: delivery concentrated in-market across the month, including on every one of the six new campaigns in the rebuilt structure. As those campaigns scale into August, the next step is hardening the underlying targeting configuration, presence-only targeting and an expanded location-exclusion list, so the concentration holds on its own rather than depending on the current campaign mix.

Separately, the legacy campaigns that spent $5,883 for zero applications sent 84% of their clicks to pages outside the application flow; the rebuilt campaigns, landing on the Spanish-language site instead, carried 13.4% of their starts through to a submitted application. Standardizing every campaign on the converting destination is the other high-leverage item for August.

## Why it happened

### Search (Bing): the account that came back mid-month

Bing spent $10,625 across three distinct windows in July, and averaging them into one monthly figure would hide the entire story.

- **July 1 to 10, dark.** No delivery, carried over from the June pause.
- **July 11 to 22, the legacy structure.** $5,883 spent, zero submitted applications.
- **July 22 to 31, the rebuilt Mexico-only structure.** $4,742 spent, 20 submitted applications at $237.12 each, all from two new campaigns.

The rebuild is what moved the number. The full-month cost per submitted application, $531.25, blends nine dead days and eleven zero-conversion days into the twenty that actually delivered, so it understates what the rebuilt structure is now doing on its own. Read the second half of July, not the average of the month, as the account's current state. August's first four days back that read: $243 per submitted application on $1,459 of spend, with two additional products now converting alongside the one that carried July.

Two campaigns produced every July conversion: the TradingView-themed campaign contributed 18 of the 20 applications, and a second new campaign contributed the remaining 2.

**Campaign continuity.** June's deck named Platform Intercept the account's efficiency leader at $285 per submitted application and highlighted the AO Generic policy-test campaign at 27 applications near $500 each, recommending a rebalance toward Platform Intercept. Both campaigns carried into July's legacy structure and produced zero applications there before retiring into the rebuilt Mexico-only structure on July 22. July's economics did not follow those campaigns forward; the rebuild superseded the rebalance recommendation, and the TradingView campaign is now the account's leader, carrying 18 of July's 20 applications and 13.4% of its own starts through to submission.

**Why the legacy campaigns converted at zero.** The diagnostic that separates the two structures is where the clicks landed, not just when they ran. The legacy campaigns sent 84% of their July clicks to pages outside the application flow, mainly the corporate overview page and two older landing pages, and every one of their 207 application starts stopped at the second step of the funnel with no exceptions. The rebuilt campaigns landed on the Spanish-language site instead, and the TradingView campaign alone carried 13.4% of its starts through to a submitted application. The landing destination and the keyword intent behind each campaign changed at the same time, so this is not a proven single cause, but the step data itself is unambiguous: the loss sits in the handoff from a started application to a submitted one, not in top-of-funnel volume.

**Mexico-only delivery: the June correction held.** June's deck committed to redirecting roughly $8.6K a month of spend that had been serving outside Mexico back into the market. That correction carried into July: delivery concentrated in-market across the month, including on every one of the six new campaigns in the rebuilt structure. As those campaigns scale into August, the next step is hardening the configuration behind that result, presence-only targeting and an expanded location-exclusion list, so the concentration holds on its own rather than depending on the current campaign mix.

### Programmatic: Quantcast, reach at a viewability cost

Quantcast spent $39,240 against 50,483,289 impressions and 19,188 clicks, all within Mexico; the geo cut confirms 100.00% of spend and impressions delivered in-market. June's deck delivered a 49-site blocklist and recommended a campaign-level viewability floor going forward. July's refreshed list covers 66 domains, and viewability improved to 54.45% from June's 51.3%, a 3.15-point gain that still sits under the 70% floor the account works against. The campaign-level viewability floor recommended in June has not yet been implemented and carries forward into August.

One site accounts for a disproportionate share of the remaining gap on its own. Spend on tvazteca.com grew to $3,451.61 in July while viewability on that domain held near 9.6%, the second consecutive month the pattern has repeated. If the client actions a single exclusion this month, that is the one.

Quantcast's result volume this month (15) is too low to support a cost-per-result figure, so the channel stays in the deck as an awareness and reach story, not a conversion story.

### Programmatic: Azerion, steady on efficiency, viewability passing

Azerion spent $37,509 against 7,577,455 impressions and 12,915 clicks, and the vendor reports 41 submitted applications at $914.85 each. These are figures from the vendor's own tracking rather than from SA360, the source of record behind Bing's count, and the deck states that distinction every time the number appears.

June's viewability recommendation is one of the few from that deck that fully landed: display rose from 68.5% in June to 82.47% in July, and native came in at 77.34%, both now clear of the 70% floor.

Ad-set economics rotated month to month. June flagged Conversion_Commodities for reallocation after it spent $4,128 with zero applications, and named Global Market the cost-per-application leader at $412. July reversed both: Conversion_Commodities converted 6 applications at $500 each, while Global Market fell to 2 applications at $1,660. The month's ad-set leader was Conversion_Instruments, with 9 applications at roughly $339. At Azerion's monthly volume, ad-set-level winners rotate faster than a reallocation decision can act on them, so the channel reads better at the overall monthly figure, $914.85 per vendor-reported application and stable, than by chasing last month's ad-set leader. This also closes out June's Conversion_Commodities reallocation call; the ad set converted well this month on its own.

Country-level delivery confirmation, requested from the vendor in early July, is still pending. That is a data-completeness item rather than a delivery concern, and certification of the country-level detail remains open.

### Social (Meta): spend and delivery only this month

Meta spent $8,027 against 4,250,494 impressions and 74,489 link clicks, a 1.75% click-through rate (June: 2.06%) and roughly $1.89 CPM. Reach on the largest campaign came to 2,251,346; reach is stated at the largest campaign because a de-duplicated account-level reach was not available from the reporting API this cycle, and campaign reach figures are never summed. Delivery held to Mexico across every campaign.

Placement mix shifted toward Instagram: 10.2% of July delivery ran there, up from 0.3% in June, alongside Facebook at 89.2% and small shares on Audience Network, Threads and Messenger. That answers the one specific ask the client made in the June review. The other ask, narrowing targeting away from the 55-and-older audience, was not implemented; targeting still runs 18 to 65 with Meta's own audience expansion turned on.

This month's Meta reporting covers spend and delivery only: impressions, reach, frequency, link clicks, click-through rate, cost per click, cost per thousand impressions, landing-page views, placements and geo. Meta's platform reports 117 submitted-application pixel fires in July; that figure is Meta-reported and unvalidated, is not compared to Bing's SA360-reported count, and does not carry a cost-per-application figure of its own.

### Site traffic (GA4, Mexico): context, not conversion

Mexico sessions came to 7,310 in July, down from June's 9,236, and unique visitors came to 3,981. Read alongside Bing's own month: the account was dark for the first ten days and running a non-converting legacy structure for the next eleven, so a large share of July's session dip tracks the search channel's own gap rather than a broader traffic decline. Property-wide, the headline session count looks worse than the underlying pattern because one unattributed traffic bucket collapsed sharply month over month; sessions outside that bucket actually grew.

The tracking gap that limited what GA4 could confirm this month is fixed. The event that should record a submitted application as a key event started counting correctly on the last day of July and has held at full capture every day since. July still has to be reported on the SA360 count because the fix landed with one day of the month left. GA4 cannot independently corroborate what Bing reports until August.

June's deck also committed to linking the Bing account into GA4, with a note that paid search would restate larger once linked and that the restatement would be flagged when it landed. Completion of that link has not yet been verified; the restatement cannot happen until it is, which is expected alongside August's key-event corroboration.

Venezuela's session volume from Mexico's Spanish-language site fell sharply in July, tracking the same legacy-campaign pause behind Bing's in-market concentration this month. That is a delivery-volume effect of the pause, the same caveat as the Mexico-only note above: it reflects this month's campaign mix, not a change to the underlying targeting configuration on its own.

## What to do next

1. **Harden the Mexico targeting configuration as the rebuilt campaigns scale.** July held the in-market concentration June's correction promised. The next step is presence-only targeting and an expanded location-exclusion list on every enabled campaign, so the concentration holds through August's higher volume rather than depending on the current campaign mix.
2. **Standardize every Bing campaign on the converting landing destination.** The legacy campaigns sent 84% of clicks to pages outside the application flow and produced zero of their 207 application starts as submissions; the rebuilt campaigns landing on the Spanish-language site carried 13.4% of starts through to submission. Review the handoff from the legacy landing pages to the application flow and bring every active campaign onto the destination that is converting.
3. **Turn on bidding feedback for the Bing conversion goals.** Every conversion goal in the account is still excluded from bidding, and every campaign runs on manual bids, so July's $237.12 cost per submitted application on the rebuilt structure was earned with no automated signal at all. Enabling the goals for bidding gives the platform the data it needs to improve on that number without manual tuning.
4. **Apply the refreshed 66-site Quantcast disallow list, leading with tvazteca.com, and implement the campaign-level viewability floor recommended in June.** Spend on tvazteca.com grew to $3,451.61 in July at roughly 9.6% viewability, the second month running, and viewability overall sits at 54.45% against the 70% target despite July's 3.15-point gain. The floor recommendation has not yet gone in and remains the structural fix.
5. **Judge Azerion at the channel level and close out the pending vendor confirmation.** Ad-set winners rotated month to month, Conversion_Commodities up, Global Market down, so hold pace on the channel's stable $914.85 vendor-reported figure rather than reallocating off one month's ad-set data. Separately, follow up on the country-level delivery confirmation still pending with the vendor since early July.
6. **Complete the Bing-to-GA4 account link.** This closes out the restatement June's deck promised and lines up with August, the first month GA4 can also corroborate Bing's submitted-application count independently.
