## The story in one breath

June was the biggest media month of the year, and the channels we can measure held their efficiency through a 2x scale-up. Traffic is arriving in volume. The one constraint is the start-to-submit step in the funnel, a conversion problem we solve jointly with your team, not a traffic or measurement problem. We used June to prove the measurement is clean, land the Azerion start-quality fix, and map exactly where the next dollar goes on each channel.

## Three things they must remember

1. Cost per submitted application held in the $350-$420 band through a 2x scale-up. That efficiency hold is the core result of the quarter.
2. Traffic quadrupled quarter over quarter; the funnel's start-to-submit step is the single constraint, and it sits downstream of media.
3. Every channel has a specific, scoped July move: conversion objective on social, ad-rank work on search, viewability floor on display, full Native read.

## Posture notes (hold these all the way through)

- **This is the US-regulated entity.** Forex and FX language is fine here. No masking. The care is around any reproduced ad claim: no "only," "best," "guaranteed safe," no income promises. You are presenting performance, so this rarely comes up, but do not ad-lib a creative claim.
- **Never offer to cut spend.** Media's mandate is qualified traffic and submitted applications at controlled cost, and it delivered. The move is convert better, not spend less.
- **Keep conversion definitions separate.** Meta's 136 are pixel events, mostly starts, on a traffic objective. Google's 67 are submitted applications. Never add them. Never quote a blended CPA.
- **The funnel constraint is a joint fix, not a client failure.** Frame the start-to-submit gap as shared roadmap work. You bring the traffic, the conversion step is built together.
- **Native is intentionally small.** Do not over-read $3,645. The full pilot reports in July.

## Slide-by-slide

### Slide 1 — Cover
**Say:** "Q3 quarter-in-review plus June detail. I'll walk the quarter, then each channel, then the July priorities. The short version is the money worked where we can measure it, and the one thing to fix is a conversion step, not the media."

### Slide 2 — Summary, blended view
**Say:** June live applications up 6% to 306, submissions up 7%, approval rate steady at 45%. Working media at $117K, the biggest GCG month of the year, with the Native pilot adding a fifth channel. The headline: sessions grew 30% while application starts stayed flat, so conversion, not volume, is the constraint.
**Emphasize:** The last bullet. Q2 closed 14% below Q1 on applications while media scaled 5x, and the dilution sits in the start-to-submit step. That is the whole deck in one line.
**If they ask "why is cost per funded up 50%?":** Blended organic and paid, and the funnel softened at the start-to-submit step while media scaled. Approval and funding rates actually held. I'll show the funnel math on slide 5.

### Slide 3 — Q3 spend
**Say:** $251K for the quarter, about 16% of the annual budget, 19% year to date. Balanced mix, no channel above 27% of June spend. Paid social led June growth, then search, then the reach line, and Native launched.
**Emphasize:** A balanced five-channel program with budget runway. We are under a fifth of the annual spend.

### Slide 4 — Q3 applications
**Say:** 332 measured submitted applications for the quarter, June the best month at 125, and cost per submitted application held in the $350-$420 band through a 2x scale-up. That hold is the core result. Azerion returned to form: 58 applications, up 35% on a 12% budget increase, at $510, down 17%.
**Emphasize:** June's search scale-up surfaced the account's rank ceiling. 64-76% of impressions are lost to ad rank, only 9-13% to budget. The next dollar goes to bids and quality, not budget. That is a precise, useful finding.
**If they ask about the Meta column:** Those are pixel events, mostly application starts, on a traffic objective. Not comparable to submitted applications, so we footnote them and never add them to the measured total.

### Slide 5 — Q1 vs Q2 funnel  *(the pivotal slide, slow down)*
**Say:** Straight at it. Traffic quadrupled quarter over quarter, application starts grew 14%, submissions fell 14%. Each funnel stage dampened the volume the media bought: the start rate diluted from 17% to 5%, the submit rate from 17% to 13%. But approval, funding, and activation held steady, and those run on the application-review and account journey downstream of media.
**Emphasize:** Media's mandate is qualified traffic and submitted applications at controlled unit cost, and that held. Converting starts into submissions is the joint funnel work on the July roadmap, and it is where the next accounts come from.
**If they say "we spent 5x for the same funded accounts":** Correct, and the reason is specific and fixable. Funded held flat because the start-to-submit rate compressed as we scaled traffic. That step is a form-and-flow problem we fix together, not a media efficiency problem. Media delivered the traffic and the submitted apps at a cost that held.

### Slide 6 — Site traffic, Spanish-language audience
**Say:** The Spanish-language base held near 70K sessions from January through May and rose 15% in June with the scale-up, unique visitors up 40%. Two things this proves: media is expanding the audience, and the measurement is working. Site analytics capture 57% of paid-social clicks and search reconciles exactly.
**Emphasize:** The gap between traffic growth and application growth is a conversion problem, not a traffic or measurement problem. This slide rules out the other two explanations.

### Slide 7 — Executive summary
**Say:** The channel scorecard. $117K across five lines. Azerion led conversion at 58 applications for $510. Search added 67 at $336. Native entered the market. The search scale-up surfaced the rank ceiling, paid social scaled 151% with healthy delivery, the reach line needs the viewability blocklist.
**Emphasize:** The footnote: different events per channel, never summed. Meta's 136 are starts, Quantcast's are view-through.

### Slide 8 — Continued summary, three moves
**Say:** Three moves for July. Search efficiency at scale: the account is rank-limited, so more budget at current ad rank buys worse auctions; the fix is bid, quality, and RSA work. Paid social conversion measurement: delivery is healthy but the objective is still traffic, so we shift it to conversions. And the Native pilot: too small in June for a real read, full report in July.
**If they ask "why not just add search budget?":** Because the data says budget is not the limiter. Campaigns lose 64-76% of impressions to ad rank and only 9-13% to budget. We earn the rank first, then scale into the impression share it opens. Budget without rank work buys lower-quality auctions.

### Slide 9 — Google Ads performance
**Say:** 67 submitted applications at $336. The scale-up mapped the rank ceiling. All 67 are true submitted applications, definition unchanged. TrackB is the efficiency core: Platform at $264, Authority at $297.
**Emphasize:** Impression share tells the story. Campaigns lose 64-76% to ad rank, only 9-13% to budget. Brand holds just 11% of its own auctions. There is a large runway here that budget alone cannot buy.
**If they ask about May being better (76 at $200):** May was the efficiency peak. June's larger budget pushed into the auction ceiling. That is not a decline in the work, it is the account telling us where the limit is, and the limit is ad rank, which we can move.

### Slide 10 — Google read and recommendations
**Say:** Ad-rank work comes before added budget. Bid up the proven TrackB terms, refresh RSAs to lift ad strength, rebuild quality score on the Trust track, and defend Brand, which sits at 11% impression share and leaves the highest-intent auctions to competitors. Hold June budget while rank recovers, then scale into the impression share it opens.

### Slide 11 — Meta performance
**Say:** Spend scaled 2.5x with clean delivery. Landing-page views ran at 70.6% of link clicks, up from May, and site analytics independently recorded 89 application starts from paid social. The channel still runs the Q2 traffic objective, so its 136 pixel events are mostly starts.
**Emphasize:** 1.53M people reached at 2.0 frequency, the largest Spanish-language reach of any GCG channel with verified site arrival. The conversion-objective shift is the unlock.

### Slide 12 — Meta read and recommendations
**Say:** Make the objective match the goal. The channel does what a traffic objective asks: low-cost verified site visitors at scale, 89 measured starts. What it does not do yet is optimize toward applications, because the objective never asked it to. As an upper-funnel channel, its submitted apps largely credit to the search and direct visits that close them.
**Emphasize:** Every delivery signal supports the shift: stable CPM, improving click-to-view rate, healthy capture, a four-month retargeting pool. July is the change.
**If they ask "is $30K on Meta worth it for 136 starts?":** Meta is the top of the funnel, not the closer. It reached 1.5M Spanish-language users and put 89 verified starts into the site. In July we point it at applications and judge it on submissions. Reading it as a direct-response channel today undersells what it is doing.

### Slide 13 — Quantcast performance and read
**Say:** A deliberate reach month. 37.2M impressions to 23.2M devices at a flat $0.82 CPM. Viewability fell to 46.9%, well under the 70% standard. We built an 18-site blocklist covering 32% of spend, yahoo, clarin, weather, genius, zillow among them, and recommend a viewability floor. Results rose to 15, but 13 are view-through, so directional only.
**Emphasize:** The list ships with this report. Applying it plus a floor moves the line toward the standard without giving up the reach efficiency.

### Slide 14 — Azerion performance and read
**Say:** 58 applications, up 35% on a 12% budget increase, at $510, down 17%. The strongest converter of the June program. And here is the win from last month: application starts fell from 1,398 to 447 while completion improved from 3.1% to 13.0%. Fewer, better-qualified starts. That is the start-to-submit fix May called for, and it landed.
**Emphasize:** Delivery confirmed US-only on a state-level geo report. Trusted Broker and Broker 1 are the concentration targets. Note the Native pilot line: intentionally small first flight, full read in July.
**If they ask about viewability at 58.8%:** Under the 70% standard, and improving. The 300x600 format converts best, 728x90 worst, so the fix is format and placement concentration.

### Slide 15 — Cross-channel priorities and next steps
**Say:** Three priorities. Convert the traffic: shift paid social to the conversion objective and put the start-to-submit step on the joint roadmap. Earn the rank: run the ad-rank program on search, defend Brand, then scale into recovered impression share. Guard the quality: carry the Azerion start-quality fix forward, hold the reach line at pace once the blocklist and floor land.
**Land it:** "July priorities: the conversion objective on social, ad-rank work on search, the viewability floor on display. Q3 built the reach, proved the measurement, and mapped the ceiling on every channel. July converts it."

## Questions you should expect, and the one-line answer

- **"We scaled 5x and funded accounts are flat. Why?"** The start-to-submit rate compressed as traffic grew. Media delivered qualified traffic and submitted apps at a held cost. Converting starts to submissions is the joint funnel fix.
- **"Google got more expensive in June. Is it declining?"** No. It hit the ad-rank ceiling. Budget is not the limiter, rank is, and rank we can move with bids, quality, and RSAs.
- **"Is $30K on Meta justified?"** It is the top of the funnel: 1.5M reach, 89 verified starts. In July it optimizes toward applications and we judge it on submissions.
- **"Why so many footnoted conversions?"** Each channel reports a different event. We never blend. Google's 67 are real submitted applications; the rest are labeled for what they are.
- **"What is Native doing?"** A deliberately small first flight. Full pilot read comes with July, judged on cost per submitted application.
