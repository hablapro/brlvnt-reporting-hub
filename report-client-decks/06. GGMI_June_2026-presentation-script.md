# GGMI (LATAM) — June 2026 / Q3 FY2026 Review
## Presenter Script — Berelvant → FOREX.com / StoneX leadership

19 slides. Plan for 25-30 minutes with questions. Read the posture notes once before you walk in.

---

## The story in one breath

June was the biggest media month of the year, and everywhere we can measure efficiency, it held or improved as we scaled. The blended funnel came in below plan, but the shortfall sits in two places outside paid-media execution: a market-volatility tailwind that carried the early year and has now reversed, and an organic-search decline that is a site-content problem. We spent June finding and fixing the real constraints, so H2 scales on clean delivery.

## Three things they must remember

1. Paid-driven applications grew every month of Q3, and cost per submitted application held near $650 through a 53% spend increase.
2. The gap to plan is the non-paid base, the volatility reversal plus organic decline, not paid-media execution.
3. July closes the measurement (GA4 link, placement audit, geo confirmation) and opens the SEO workstream, so H2 compounds instead of renting traffic.

## Posture notes (hold these all the way through)

- **Geo number is $8,612.** That is the figure already communicated to the client. Never introduce the larger internal count. If pressed: "acknowledged, corrected, July confirms."
- **Never offer to cut spend.** The move is always "concentrate on the proven converters and finish the measurement." Paid media is the engagement.
- **Keep conversion definitions separate.** If asked for one apps total, give the per-channel numbers and the blended figure with its caveat. Never quote a blended CPA.
- **Internal ownership stays out of the room.** Who set up the geo or the pixel is not the client's meeting. Everything is framed as work in progress.
- When a slide is a hard one (7, 8, 9, 14), slow down. The honesty is the credibility. You are the one who found the problem and scoped the fix.

## Slide-by-slide

### Slide 1 — Cover
**Say:** "This is the Q3 quarter-in-review plus the June detail. I'll walk the quarter first, then go channel by channel, then land on the July priorities." Set the frame that this is a review and a plan, not just a scorecard.

### Slide 2 — Summary, blended view
**Say:** June submitted applications at 684, approval rate recovered to 31% (best since February), working media at $120K, the biggest month of the year. Approval improving while we scaled is the quality signal.
**Emphasize:** The bullet that paid-driven applications grew every month of Q3. That is the line the rest of the deck defends.
**If they ask "why is cost per funded up 72% and apps down?":** These rows are blended organic and paid, and the base is mostly organic. The cost-per rows climb because the funnel softened while media scaled, and the softening is downstream of media. I'll show you exactly where it sits on slides 4 through 7.

### Slide 3 — Q3 spend
**Say:** $245K in working media for the quarter, roughly 24% of the annual budget. The mix rebalanced as we scaled: no single channel above 29% of June spend. A balanced four-channel program is more durable than leaning on one line.
**If they ask about pace:** +70% into May, +53% into June, June at 2.6x April. We scaled deliberately and held efficiency where we can measure it, which is what earns the next stage.

### Slide 4 — Performance tracks market volatility
**Say:** This is the single most important context slide. GGMI's acquisition funnel and active base move with the markets. Applications track gold at 0.91, actives track the VIX at 0.94. When gold and volatility faded, volume fell 78% from the January peak.
**Emphasize:** The Apr-Jun miss versus budget is largely the volatility tailwind reversing, not a change in how the media performed.
**If they push on correlation:** These are our own coefficients off your Budget-vs-Actual dashboard against public gold and VIX data. It is a strong, consistent relationship across the half-year, and it explains the shape of the funnel better than any media variable does.

### Slide 5 — Acquisition funnel vs budget
**Say:** Downstream outcomes beat budget through March on the volatility wave. Applications ran under budget even then, and the gap widened as gold and the VIX normalized. The strategic read: treat volatility as the acquisition dial, spend hard into VIX spikes, shift to activation and retention when it is calm like now.
**If they ask "so what do we do in a calm market?":** Lead Mexico creative with the gold intent that still converts, reactivate the January-March sign-ups with display, and hold aggressive acquisition for the next VIX spike. This is a media plan, not a wait.

### Slide 6 — Q3 applications
**Say:** 103 blended applications in June, cost per application down from $2,315 in April to $1,169 and held there through a 53% spend increase. Search and programmatic carried the measured volume: 181 submitted applications across the quarter near $650 each.
**Emphasize:** Growth was bought at improving efficiency, not by paying more per outcome.
**If they ask about Meta showing "Held":** Meta is on a traffic objective and its conversion reporting is under validation. We do not blend it into a number we cannot stand behind. More on slide 14.

### Slide 7 — Blended outcomes vs plan  *(hard slide, slow down)*
**Say:** Straight at it: blended submissions ran about 45% under plan and declined through the half-year. Two forces overlap. The volatility tailwind that carried the early-year baseline reversed, which is slides 4 and 5. And the site's organic capture declined, which is slides 8 and 9. The funded step, down 56% to plan, is the application-to-funded gap your onboarding roadmap is built to fix.
**Emphasize:** Paid-driven applications grew every month of Q3 while this blended line fell. The shortfall is the non-paid base and the downstream funnel, not paid delivery.
**If they say "this looks bad":** It is an honest read of a blended number, and the honest read is what tells you where to act. The paid program grew and held efficiency. The two fixable causes are named, and both have owners: the SEO workstream and your onboarding roadmap, the lower deposit minimum, stablecoin, Persona KYC.

### Slide 8 — Site traffic, six-month view
**Say:** June sessions rose 61% on the media scale-up, unique visitors up 26% since January. Media proved it can move traffic on demand. But be precise about what June was: of the 3,485 new sessions versus May, paid search added 2,601, paid social 537, display 274. Organic added 94.
**Emphasize:** The recovery is real and it is bought. Until organic recovers, total traffic tracks spend rather than compounding on top of it.

### Slide 9 — Organic search, the structural issue  *(hard slide)*
**Say:** Organic search fell 75% in six months, and media cannot buy it back. The cause is specific: dated news and analysis content aged out with nothing replacing it, and mid-funnel commercial pages slid in rank while brand improved. Confirmed independently in Search Console, and it spans every country the Spanish site serves.
**Emphasize:** This is a site-content issue, not a paid-media issue, and it needs its own workstream.
**If they ask "is this on you?":** This is the content and SEO layer of the site, separate from the paid program. We found it, we diagnosed it page by page, and we are recommending the workstream to fix it. That is the agency doing its job across the whole picture, not just the channels we buy.
**Framing to land:** Position the SEO workstream as the highest-leverage new investment, not an apology.

### Slide 10 — Executive summary
**Say:** The channel scorecard. $120K across four channels. Search produced 50 submitted applications at $513, up from 33 in May. Azerion held efficiency at 42 applications through a 20% budget increase. We are about 31% into the annual budget, so H2 has room to scale once the quality fixes land.
**Emphasize:** The footnote is deliberate: conversions use different events per channel and are never summed. We report each channel on its own truth.

### Slide 11 — Continued summary, three findings
**Say:** Three things shape July. Organic is down 75% and no budget fixes it, so we open the SEO workstream. Meta scaled hard but click quality dropped as delivery moved into low-cost in-app inventory, so its conversions stay on hold until the placement audit. And $8,612 of June search served outside Mexico; Mexico-only targeting is now applied and July confirms the correction.
**If they ask about the geo number:** $8,612 of June delivery served outside Mexico, led by Spain and Venezuela. The targeting is corrected and in place now. July delivery is the confirmation. Then move on. Do not expand the number.

### Slide 12 — Bing Ads performance
**Say:** 50 submitted applications at $513, up from 33 at $484 in May. Conversions rose 52% while spend rose 61%, so the CPA barely moved through the scale-up. Platform Intercept is the efficiency leader at $285.
**Emphasize:** Search is the most efficient measured converter in the account, and it held that efficiency while scaling 61%.
**If they ask about CPC rising to $1.19:** That is the shift into the new campaign themes. The mix has room to rebalance toward Platform Intercept, which is where the $285 CPA lives.

### Slide 13 — Bing read and recommendations
**Say:** Concentrate every search dollar in Mexico. June was the last month on the old geo setup. With Mexico-only targeting in place, that $8,612 concentrates in-market, which should improve effective Mexico CPA immediately. Rebalance toward the proven converters and link the search account in GA4 so July attributes paid search fully.

### Slide 14 — Meta performance  *(handle with care)*
**Say:** 19.8M impressions at a $1.31 CPM, all in Mexico. The June question is click quality. This account converted platform clicks into measured site visits at 15.7% in February and 0.19% in June. Same pixel, same site, same market. The February benchmark isolates inventory quality as the cause.
**Emphasize:** The platform reports 86 conversions, but 67 came from one retargeting campaign with only 126 landing-page views, which is not a credible application rate. We are holding conversion reporting until the placement audit and pixel review complete. Delivery metrics are unaffected.
**If they ask "why hold conversions you could report?":** Because reporting a number we cannot defend is worse than holding it. We would rather show you clean applications in July than book 86 conversions we do not believe. That discipline protects both of us.

### Slide 15 — Meta read and recommendations
**Say:** Audit the placements before judging the channel. Delivery skewed into low-cost in-app inventory, 63% of spend to users 55 and older. The placement audit and exclusions are the highest-value July action here. Judge the channel on measured site sessions until validation completes, then reinstate conversions.

### Slide 16 — Azerion performance
**Say:** 42 submitted applications at $834, from 37 at $792 in May. Efficiency roughly held at a 20% higher budget. Application starts nearly doubled, so the upper funnel stayed active. Global Market converts at $412, Experience carries the most volume.
**Emphasize:** Conversion_Commodities spent $4,128 with zero applications and is the first reallocation candidate. Specific, actionable, shows we manage the line.

### Slide 17 — Azerion read and recommendations
**Say:** Hold the pace, confirm the delivery detail. Second straight month of scaled volume with efficiency holding within 6%. Viewability at 68.5%, just under the 70% standard. We have asked the vendor for country-level delivery plus funnel and creative breakdowns, and Mexico-only certification is pending that reply.
**If they ask "is Azerion in-market?":** Delivery certification is pending the vendor's country-level report, which we requested in early July. We are holding budget pace until it arrives rather than assuming.

### Slide 18 — Quantcast performance and read
**Say:** A reach month on purpose. 42M impressions and 18.2M devices as the CPM fell by half to $0.81. Scaling reach at that price pulled in low-viewability inventory, 51% against the 70% standard. We built a 49-site blocklist covering 32% of spend and recommend a viewability floor. All 11 conversions are view-through, so treat them as directional, not proven.
**Emphasize:** We name the quality bill ourselves and bring the fix in the same breath. That is the pattern for the whole deck.

### Slide 19 — Cross-channel priorities and next steps
**Say:** Three priorities. Concentrate in-market: Mexico-only search is applied, July confirms $8.6K a month redirected. Finish the measurement: link search in GA4, complete the placement audit. Start the SEO workstream for the Spanish site. Hold the mix pending the fixes, then let clean July attribution guide allocation.
**Land it:** "Concentrate spend on measurable, in-market delivery while the quality fixes land. June proved the program can scale and hold efficiency. July closes the measurement and starts the compounding."

## Questions you should expect, and the one-line answer

- **"Apps are down. Why keep spending?"** Paid-driven apps grew every month and held cost. The misses are the non-paid base. Concentrate on the proven converters, finish the measurement, and H2 scales.
- **"Is the volatility story an excuse?"** It is a 0.91 and 0.94 correlation across six months off your own dashboard. It predicts the funnel better than any media variable.
- **"When does this turn?"** July closes the measurement and starts the SEO and funnel workstreams. That is when traffic compounds instead of tracking spend.
- **"Why so many held or footnoted conversions?"** Each channel reports a different event. We hold what we cannot defend and never blend. You get numbers you can trust.
